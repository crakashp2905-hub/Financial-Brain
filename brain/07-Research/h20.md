# h20

**Momentum held for a quarter, out of sample, against its own null.** Pre-registered in
`hypotheses/h20_momentum_at_its_own_horizon.toml`.

This is the confirmatory test for the one result that has ever come out net positive with a
mechanism behind it, and the pre-registration exists because the result came out of a **search**:
nine holding periods across five features, forty-five combinations, examined *after* seeing the
h = 20 results.

## What the search found

`mom_12_1`, top quintile against the equal-weighted universe, costed per rebalance on the book's
own liquidity mix:

| h | IC t | gross/period | turnover | net/period | net t |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 | +6.15 | +0.215% | 12% | +0.114% | +2.51 |
| 20 | +3.78 | +0.758% | 27% | +0.541% | +2.63 |
| **40** | **+3.95** | **+1.501%** | **36%** | **+1.202%** | **+2.85** |
| 60 | +3.44 | +1.886% | 45% | +1.521% | +2.53 |
| 250 | +2.03 | +6.650% | 82% | +5.987% | +1.86 |

Net positive at every horizon, peaking at h = 40 at roughly **+7.6% a year** of excess. The IC
decays with horizon and the cost amortises with it; net is their product and peaks in the middle,
which is what a real effect against a real cost looks like and the opposite of the single-point
spike fitting produces.

All forty-five are recorded in `evaluation_runs`, which took the ledger from 81 trials to 126 and
the Bonferroni bar to **|t| > 3.54**; it has since reached **3.59**. **+2.85 does not clear it.**

## Against a correctly specified null

The first cross-sectional null said `mom_12_1` scored **higher on noise** than on real data -
separation −0.05 - which read at face value retires the most replicated anomaly in the literature.
It was wrong: `synthetic.build` gave each synthetic name the drift of the real name it was matched
to, and persistent per-name drift *is* cross-sectional return persistence. The panel contained the
alternative hypothesis. See [[One bad control from writing off momentum]].

With `drift="common"`, momentum's separation is **+3.09** on net t and **+3.12** on alpha.

## Risk-adjusted, and on the honest benchmark

| benchmark | beta | alpha | alpha t |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| equal-weighted universe, h=40 | 0.982 | +1.239% | +3.00 |
| **Nifty 500, h=40** | **1.208** | **+1.554%** | **+1.95** |

A bigger alpha with a worse t, because a momentum book is poorly explained by a cap-weighted index
and the residual variance grows. The most investable benchmark gives the weakest statistic, which
is the direction that matters.

## And it may not be a second bet

`dist_52w_high` is the other survivor and the two are **33% overlapping at correlation +0.51**.
Combining them improves the t-statistic by **+0.04**. See
[[Per-stock selection loses to not selecting]] for the machinery.

## What is owed before this means anything

1. an out-of-sample test on rebalances after the sweep's cutoff, through
   `registry.test(mode="out_of_sample")`, which refuses until `MIN_OOS_DATES` exist - at h = 40
   that is a real wait and the wait is the point;
2. the walk-forward and regime gates, because a momentum book held 40 sessions in a market that
   rose over the sample is exposed to exactly the drift the excess-over-universe benchmark is
   meant to remove and may not fully;
3. a capacity study at real size: 36% turnover on 40 names with 26.5% in small caps is where the
   square-root impact law bites and the bucket table stops being adequate.

## Status

**Not tested.** The in-sample search is recorded, the confirmatory test is registered, and nothing
here is tradeable. The honest one-line summary is that the closest result in this project's history
is half a t-statistic short of its own bar, on a search that has not been confirmed.
