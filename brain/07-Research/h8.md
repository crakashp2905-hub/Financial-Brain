## Result: REJECT

    dealing_60d   REJECT   trial 28   IC -0.002   t -0.45   DSR 0.00
                  net/period -0.20%   turnover 35%   sign held in 50% of years

Against h5's **t = -0.35**, essentially unchanged and marginally worse. The branch below
that fired is the second one, and the prior stated in advance was the right one.

**Why so little moved.** The compliance certificates ran at roughly 8,500 a year across
2017-2024 - a near-constant term present for most names in most windows. A constant
contributes nothing to a *cross-sectional rank*, so removing a third of the feature by
volume moved the statistic by 0.1 of a t. Noise that does not discriminate is not the
noise that was hurting it.

**What it settles.** SAST Reg. 29 is now 90% of the feature, and counting those filings
without reading their direction carries no signal. Direction is genuinely the remaining
variable, and that claim is now earned rather than assumed - which is the whole reason to
run the cheap test before paying for 95,751 attachment parses.

Stated in advance, so neither outcome can be explained after the fact:

* **If it works** - the earlier rejection was a data-quality artifact, and the lesson is
  about auditing what a feature contains before believing what it measures.
* **If it still fails** - the count is genuinely blunt, and direction is the one remaining
  variable. Only then is parsing 95,751 SAST attachments worth its cost.
* **The prior is weaker than h5's, not stronger.** Removing noise raises signal-to-noise;
  it cannot manufacture a signal that was never there. The surviving 60.5% still counts an
  acquisition and a disposal identically.
