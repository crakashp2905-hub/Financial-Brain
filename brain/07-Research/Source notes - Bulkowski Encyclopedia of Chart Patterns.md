# Source notes: Bulkowski, Encyclopedia of Chart Patterns (Wiley, 2000)

Read in full (345 pages, 47 pattern chapters) on 2026-11-07. Paraphrased working notes with the book's own reported
statistics recorded as **claims about US stocks in 1991-96**, not as evidence for India or for today. The cross-source
synthesis and the testing plan are in [[Chart patterns and candles - what is testable and how]]; a machine-readable
catalogue is in `docs/sources/pattern-catalogue.v1.json`.


## Method of the book (critical for how to treat its numbers)
- Data: 500 US stocks, daily bars, mid-1991 to mid-1996 (a strong bull market), plus ~300 stocks 1996-1999 for rare patterns. Over 15,000 formations found by a program plus the author's eye; patterns are identified by hand/program on charts, then statistics logged. Mostly NYSE/AMEX/NASDAQ names; stocks below $1 dropped; "unduly flat" or very volatile stocks excluded (selection).
- A pattern is only "confirmed" by a BREAKOUT: a CLOSE beyond the formation's high/low (or trendline). Statistics apply only to formations that have already broken out => conditioned on the future (what happens after confirmation), and the formation boundaries (highest high / lowest low, trendlines) are drawn knowing the whole formation.
- "Average rise/decline" = move from the breakout-day price (high or low) to the ULTIMATE high/low: the extreme before a >=20% trend change (high to low). That is a maximum-favourable-excursion measure (peak-to-trough), NOT a return you can capture; he warns the average is a best case and gives a "most likely" (modal 10%-bin) rise/decline from a frequency distribution (typically 10-15% for bullish, ~10% for bearish).
- "Failure" = breakout then price fails to move >5% in the breakout direction before reversing (a '5% failure') or it breaks out the wrong way. Failure rate under 20% is called acceptable; "measure rule" target (formation height added/subtracted at breakout) hit rate >=80% called reliable.
- Throwback (up-break returns to the boundary within 30 days) / pullback (down-break); typical 10-12 days; roughly 55-70% of formations show one.
- Score for ranking = average rise/decline x most-likely rise/decline / failure rate (ad hoc).
- Bull market 1991-96: bullish formations count more and look better; bearish ones rarer and "might just not happen".
- No transaction costs, no slippage, no multiple-testing control, no out-of-sample, no benchmark / random-entry comparison. Best used as a catalogue of definitions + a source of hypotheses, and as a list of the author's trading rules.

## Ch1 Broadening bottom (rare; 77 found): downtrend into a megaphone (>=2 minor highs and >=2 minor lows, top line up, bottom line down). Breakout up = reversal (avg rise 25%, likely ~10%, failure 2%, measure-rule hit 59%); breakout down = consolidation (avg decline 27%, likely 15-20%, failure 6%, hit 70%). Pattern is essentially bearish. Partial decline (falls from top, turns back) -> up break 80% (16/20); partial rise -> down break 67%. Trade: buy bounce off lower line (stop below minor low), short from top; measure rule = height; trade the partial rise/decline. [Small samples.]
## Ch2 Right-angled & ascending broadening (216 found): flat base, rising top line; bearish reversal; failure 34% overall (9% if you wait for a confirmed downside break); avg decline only 18%, likely ~10% -> not worth shorting; measure-rule target hit only 43% (91% if half the height used - a fitted fix). Upside break (24%): buy, expect ~20%. Pullback 72%. Lesson: wait for the confirmed break; trade the break direction.
## Ch3 Right-angled & descending broadening (82 found): flat top, falling bottom line. Breaks UP 57% / down 37% (opposite of the folklore he began with). Up: avg rise 27% (likely 20-30%, small sample), failure 19%, target hit 89%; down: avg decline 19%, likely 10-15%, failure 3%, target hit 69%. Partial decline -> up break 78%; partial rise -> down break 58% (coin flip). Volume at breakout: ~43% above prior day, fading to ~71% a week later; volume does not separate successes from failures.
## Ch4 Broadening top (189 found; megaphone after an uptrend): NOT reliably bearish. Up-break 100 (consolidation; failure 4%; avg rise 34%, likely 10-15%; target hit 75%); down-break 89 (reversal; failure 4%; avg decline 23%, likely 10-20%; target 64%). Partial rise -> down break 65%; partial decline -> up break 86%. 94% of up-breaks sit in the top third of the yearly range. Time to breakout ~1 month after formation end. Trade: buy the turn at the lower line (stop below the minor low), short the turn at the top; wait for the breakout otherwise.
## Ch5 Ascending broadening wedge (157): both lines rise, top line steeper; >=3 touches each side; breaks down 80% (reversal 75%); failure 24% (6% if you wait for a confirmed down break); avg decline 20%, likely ~10% (probably 15-20); formation-low target reached only 61%; partial rise (48% of cases) -> down break 84%; pullbacks 21%; ~4 months long, ~3 months to the low.
## Ch6 Descending broadening wedge (117): both lines fall, lower steeper; behaves as a CONSOLIDATION of an uptrend (66%) - breaks up 79%; failure 37% (trend reversals count as failures); avg rise 46% to the ultimate high (likely 20%; 40% of cases >50%); target hit 81%; volume tends to rise over time (64%); partial decline -> up break 76%; ~2.5 months long, ~6.5 months to the high. Wait for the break.
## Ch7 Bump-and-run REVERSAL BOTTOM (BARR bottom; 360 found, plentiful): "frying pan, handle on left": a down-sloping trendline (~30-45 degrees, drawn on daily highs; lead-in >= 1 month), prices fall away below it into a rounded 'bump' base, then rise and close ABOVE the trendline = buy confirmation. Failure 19% (9% if waiting for the up-break); avg rise 37% from the breakout (57% from the bump low), likely 20%; 14% of cases >90% (outsized gains lift the mean); measure rule = lead-in height added to breakout price (hit 92%; using bump height 67%); ~5 months long; throwback 38%; bump is rounded 78%, multiple bumps 14%. Volume high at formation start, bump start and breakout. Stop just below prior resistance/support; consider selling half at the old high. Also called cup-with-handle relative.
## Ch8 Bump-and-run reversal TOP (650 found): rising trendline (~30-45 deg, never flat, never >60 deg), a lead-in of >=1 month (height measured from the trendline to the highest high in the first quarter), then prices accelerate (45-60+ deg, often on good news), the 'bump' must be >= 2x the lead-in height above the trendline, round over, and CLOSE BELOW the trendline = sell. 82% reversals; failure 19% (9% waiting for confirmation); avg decline 24% from the breakout (modal 15-20%; peak-to-low 30-40%); measure rule (lead-in height subtracted from the trendline where it is crossed) hit 88%; ~7 months long; pullback 39%; largest declines when the break is near the yearly LOW; bump rounded 76%; multi-bump 26%. Practical aids: 'warning line' and 'sell line' parallel to the trendline at 1x and 2x lead-in height; sell near the bump rather than waiting for the break.
## Ch9 Cup with handle (O'Neil's pattern; 391 found "unfiltered"): cup then a small handle on the right; breakout above the right lip. Mostly a consolidation of an uptrend (302 of 391; require >=30% prior rise). Failure 26% (10% if you wait for the up-break); avg rise 38%, likely 10-20% (27% of cases >50%, only 7% double); throwback to the right lip 74% (waiting for the throwback and re-break slightly improves gain 38->39%); measure rule (cup height added to lip) hit only 49%, half-height 73%; ~7 months. Cups with a higher RIGHT lip beat higher-left-lip cups (avg 40% vs 35%). Strictly applying O'Neil's rules (9% of cases qualify) did WORSE: failure 38%, avg 34%; down-sloping handle price/volume rules hurt; handle low vs 200-day MA, breakout volume, cup depth/length: no bearing. Stop just below the handle.
## Ch10 Dead-cat bounce (244): after a sudden collapse on bad news (event decline avg 25%, usually 2 days, 80% gap down on ~15-20x volume), prices bounce (avg 19%, peak within ~19 days, bigger event = bigger bounce) then decline again: 81% fall below the event low, a further avg 15% (modal 5-25%) over ~3 months. Only 22% of gaps close in the bounce, 54% within 6 months. Long-term bearish reversal; failure 10%. Tactics: sell into the bounce; short after the bounce rounds over (cover at a trendline break; ~20% gain in his example); ignore bullish chart patterns in the same stock for 6-12 months.
## Ch11 Diamond tops/bottoms (rare: 45 bottoms, 111 tops): widening then narrowing (head-and-shoulders-like), volume diminishing; bottoms: failure 13%, avg rise 35% (likely 15%), target hit 95%; tops: failure 25%, avg decline 21% (likely 20%), target hit 79%; ~7 weeks long; breakouts: bottoms up 82%, tops down 79%; throwback 43% / pullback 59%. Wait for the close outside the boundary.
## Ch12 DOUBLE BOTTOM (key chapter). Definition he used: downtrend; two lows within 4% of each other (classic: 3%), >=10 days apart (many pros want a month; best gains when ~3 weeks apart, avg 42% vs 23% at ~4 months), a rise between them of >=10% (classic 20%) measured low to high; the second low must not undercut the first; CONFIRMATION POINT = the highest high between the two lows, and the pattern only counts once price CLOSES above it. Volume: usually higher on the left low, lower on the right, rising at the breakout.
 - Hindsight/selection finding (important): of ~1,505 twin-bottom lookalikes only 525-542 (about 36%) ever closed above the confirmation point; 980 (64%) went on to break below the right low. Buying at the second bottom had roughly a one-in-three chance of working; ALL the published statistics are on the confirmed 542 only.
 - Confirmed stats (500 stocks 1991-96): 542 formations, 69% reversals; 5% failure rate 3% (17); avg rise 40% to the ultimate high, likely 20-30% (about a third >45%, a third <15%); measure rule (height added to the confirmation point) hit 68%; ~70 days between lows; avg low-to-low difference 2%, avg rise between lows 19%; throwback to the breakout price 68% (avg 11 days) -> he suggests waiting for the throwback and buying when price turns up again; ~43 days from the right low to the breakout; ~7 months to the ultimate high; gains similar whether the breakout is near the yearly low (42%), centre (38%) or high (41%); left-low volume higher in 58%; volume at the breakout day 165% of the prior day, back to 82% a week later; throwback likelihood unrelated to breakout volume (65%).
 - Tactics: wait for the close above the confirmation point; consider waiting for the throwback; sell near the measure-rule target (only 68% reach it); for longer holds rely on fundamentals.
## Ch13 DOUBLE TOP: uptrend (3-6 months) into two peaks within 3% of each other, a decline between them of >=10% (classic 20%; deeper = better), peaks a few weeks to a year apart (closer than ~60 days did better), CONFIRMATION POINT = the lowest low between the peaks, confirmed only when price CLOSES below it. Volume usually higher on the left peak, lower on the right.
 - Selection finding: 1,280 twin tops found; 65% (826) kept rising without ever closing below the valley - they are 'not double tops' and are EXCLUDED from the statistics. Only 454 confirmed (75% reversals).
 - Stats on confirmed: 5%-failure rate 17% (only 39% reach the full measure-rule target, 70% with half the height); avg decline 20% from the breakout, likely 10-15% (47% fell <15%); decline from the highest peak to the ultimate low avg 32%; avg valley depth 15%; left top higher in 56%; ~57 days between peaks; 39 days from the right top to the breakout; pullback to the breakout price 69% (avg 10 days); ~83 days to the ultimate low; high-volume breakouts fell further (21% vs 15%). Deeper valleys -> bigger losses (11% trough -> 15% loss; 18% trough -> 27%). Closer peaks do better.
 - Tactics: do not trade the pattern before the confirming close; consider selling just after the second top and buying back if the higher peak is exceeded; prefer tops <60 days apart and troughs >=15%; wait for the pullback and renewed decline; check fundamentals.
## Ch14 Flags and pennants (144 flags, 106 pennants; fewer than expected): a short (a few days to <=3 weeks; avg 10-11 days) counter-trend drift in the middle of a STEEP quick move ('half-mast'); flag = parallel lines, pennant = converging lines. Ignore if there is no steep prior move (~20% in ~6-8 weeks). Volume recedes (78% flags, 90% pennants), but rising volume is not a reason to reject. Failure: flags 13% up / 12% down; pennants 19% up / 34% down (avoid down-trend pennants). Move after ~ equals the move before (flags +19% avg, pennants +21% uptrend; -17% downtrend), likely 15-20%; measure rule (flagpole length projected from the end of the flag) hit 63%/58% up, 61%/52% down - be conservative. 76-84% form near the yearly high in uptrends. Throwback/pullback rare. Trade after the breakout from the boundaries; take reward-to-risk >=4:1 using the next support/resistance as the risk point.
## Ch15 Flags, HIGH and TIGHT (O'Neil: stock doubles in <=2 months then consolidates; 81 found, rare): failure 32% (17% if you wait for the break above the flag high); avg rise 63% (44% >50%, 33% >90%), likely 20-30%; ~20 days; 92% at yearly highs; ~70 days to the high; best when the prior rise is 110-140%. Receding volume helps (74% recede). Mild-filter version beats strict O'Neil (more cases, similar gains); author warns his own criteria search 'simply tunes the database'. Tactic: buy only after the breakout above the flag high.
## Ch16 Gaps: types - area/common (inside congestion; 90% close within a week; avg 6 days), breakaway (start of a trend, high volume, new extremes follow), continuation/runaway (mid-trend, stay open), exhaustion (end of trend, often unusually wide, follow continuation gaps, price stalls and the gap closes quickly), ex-dividend (ignore). Type can only be told apart afterwards from what price does next (hindsight). [Rest of chapter statistics: see digest.]
## Ch17 Hanging man (adapted candlestick for bar charts; defined as open=high=close with a much lower low, in a rising trend): the folk claim 'close near high -> 80% chance of a higher high next day' tested: best he found 55-57% (about a coin toss given upward drift). As a bearish reversal it works only a third of the time (67% broke UP). 5%-failure rates: up-breakouts 11%, down-breakouts 22%; up-break avg 40% (likely 10%), down-break avg 16% (likely 5-10%). Conclusion: not a reliable reversal signal. [Direct evidence on a Nison pattern - US stocks 1991-96, pattern defined with O=H=C only.]
## Ch17 detail (hanging man): Test of 'close near the high -> higher high next day': over 78,000 T-bars: 55.4% (close = high) up to 57.2% (close up to 25% below high) made a higher high next day (~coin toss + drift); true hanging man (open=high=close, low >=5% lower): only 44-48%. ~56,000 occurrences in 400 stocks; sampling limited to ~10 per stock. As a reversal in an uptrend: 155 qualifying, 104 (67%) kept rising. Low-volume cases failed 40% vs 18% with high volume. Author: cannot recommend trading it.
## Ch18 HEAD-AND-SHOULDERS BOTTOM (330): three troughs, centre lowest, shoulders roughly symmetric in price and time, neckline through the two rallies between them; volume highest on left shoulder/head, lower on right; breakout = close above neckline (steep necklines: use the highest rise between head and right shoulder). 85% reversals; failure 5% (18); avg rise 38% from the breakout to the ultimate high, likely 20-30%, many >90% (skews mean); measure rule (head-to-neckline depth added to the breakout) hit 83%; throwback to neckline 52% (~11 days); ~73 days; 98% break up. Slope of neckline or shoulder height does not matter statistically. Not necessary to wait for confirmation if the pattern is surely complete (he says), stop below the lower shoulder; buying a throwback raises odds. ~7 months to the ultimate high (but modal <3 months).
## Ch19 Complex H&S bottom (multiple shoulders/heads; 239): failure 6%, avg rise 37% (likely 20-30%), measure rule 82%, throwback 47%, ~3.5 months long, ~8 months to the high; down-sloping neckline better (39% vs 34%, significant); high-volume breakouts better (39% vs 32%); 74% have a near-horizontal neckline. Stop below the lowest shoulder/head; cut if price closes below the head (unless near a yearly low, then allow ~10% below the head).
## Ch20 HEAD-AND-SHOULDERS TOP (431; most popular): uptrend, three peaks with the centre highest, shoulders at similar price and equidistant from the head, neckline through the two troughs (slopes up 52%, down 43%), volume highest at the left shoulder / lowest at the right (62% decline overall); breakout = CLOSE below the neckline (steep necklines: use the higher trough low). 94% reversals; 7% failure; 98% break down; avg decline 23% from the neckline break, likely 15-20% (peak-to-low larger); measure rule (head-to-neckline height subtracted from breakout) hit 63% (alternative: height from the head to the higher trough low projected from that low: 69%); pullback to the neckline 45% (~11 days); ~62 days between shoulder peaks, ~79 to the neckline break, ~3 months to the low; not better near yearly highs vs lows. Lower right shoulder / down-sloping neckline: slightly bigger declines but NOT statistically significant. Author advises NOT waiting for the neckline break once the right shoulder has clearly formed (93% success) - NOTE this advice rests on patterns identified in hindsight. Stop just above the lower trough/neckline; add on pullbacks.
## Ch21 Complex H&S top (141): extra shoulders or double head; symmetry paramount; failure 8%, avg decline 27% (likely 20%, 10-30% spread), target hit 67% (76% with the higher-trough variant), ~83 days long, ~3.5 months to the low, pullback 64% (10 days); volume receding 63%; most break out in centre/upper yearly range. Same tactics: don't wait for the break once complete; stop above shoulder tops/head; re-short on pullbacks; if a mountain rose from a flat base, expect a return to the base.
## Ch22 Horn bottom (weekly chart): two long DOWNWARD spikes separated by one week, lows within ~$0.375 (or tiny %), overlap large, abnormally long spikes vs the prior year and clear 'visibility' to the left; 296 found; failure 11%; avg rise 37% (likely 20-30%), ~9 months to the high; volume spike higher on the left horn (146% of 25-day avg) than right (116%). Warning: a descending triangle with a spike can mimic it.
 Horn bottom extras: larger spikes (>=2x the year's typical spike) did better (43% gain, 12% failure); inside/outside-week variants and volume combinations differ by only 1-3 points (author notes such splits 'tune the database'); no measure rule; buy once identified, stop below.
## Ch23 Horn top (weekly; 188): two upward spikes a week apart, long vs the year's spikes, highs within ~$0.375; failure 16% (6% if BOTH spikes on below-average volume); avg decline 21% but likely only ~10% (40% fell <=15%); ~4.5 months to the low; avoid if it appears at the end of a long DOWNtrend that just pierced a down trendline.
## Ch24 Inside days (520 sampled, 10 per stock): the folk claim 'a big move follows an inside day' tested as 'next day range larger': failure 56% (up) / 51% (down) i.e. random (56% of next-day ranges were larger than the inside day); avg move after only 10-13% (likely 0-5%); inside-day close in the top/bottom 25% of ITS range -> same-direction break 61%/60%; close near the prior day's low -> down break 70%. Author: scant tactics; trade only in the trend direction after a close beyond the inside bar.
## Ch25 Island reversal (gap + gap back over the same price area, islands >=2 days; 245 tops, 278 bottoms): failure 13% tops / 17% bottoms; avg decline 21% (likely <=10%), avg rise 34% (likely ~20%); measure rule hit 78% / 85% (easy because islands are short); length ~40 / 31 days; pullback (tops) 65%, throwback (bottoms) 70% within ~8-9 days; ~72 days to the low / ~6 months to the high; high-volume breakouts help tops (22% vs 16%) but low volume helps bottoms (39% vs 33%). A reversal needs something to reverse (an island after consolidation fails). Wait for the pullback/throwback.
## Ch26 Measured move DOWN (swing measurement; 622 found, 88% reversals): first leg (straight-line decline from a new high; avg -25% in 54 days), corrective phase (retrace of 40-60% of the first leg; avg +16% in 39 days), second leg (avg -27% in 60 days). Failure (second leg does not undercut the first low) 22%; avg total decline 36% (peak to low; likely 25-40%) but you can only identify it once the second leg starts so the capturable part is far smaller (~20%); ~153 days; 93% start near yearly highs; only 43% of second legs are >= first leg (80% reached at 2/3 of the first leg). Short in the second leg, stop above the corrective high; careful about a down leg that is itself the correction of a bigger measured move up.
## Ch27 Measured move UP (501): mirror: first leg avg +43% in 87 days, correction 35-65% (avg -14% in 45 days), second leg +37% in 65 days; failure 23%; avg total 68% (likely 30-60%, >100% in a quarter) but the capturable second leg ~37%; 79% start in the lowest third of the yearly range (avoid near yearly highs); 57% of second legs >= first leg; retrace distribution shows NO Fibonacci clustering (38%/62%); avoid retraces >80%. Buy after the correction ends and price closes above the corrective-phase down-trendline.
## Ch28 One-day reversal (tops 235, bottoms 331): a spike day at least 2x the typical range of the prior year, close within the bottom (top)/third of the range, in a trend, usually high volume. Tops: failure 24%, avg -19% (likely 10%); bottoms: failure 17%, avg +26% (likely 10-15%); pullback 71% / throwback 61% (13-16 days); 49% of tops fell <=15%. Author: do not trade (too small and too late); use as a warning.
## Ch29 Outside day (510 sampled, 10/stock): range wider than the prior day on both sides. Up-break 268: failure 25%, avg +32%, likely <10%; down-break 242: failure 42%, avg -17%, likely <10%. Close of the outside day in its top 25% -> up break 66%; bottom 25% -> down break 62%; day-before close predicts little (46%/61%). Author: probably avoid.
## Ch30 Pipe bottom (weekly; two ADJACENT weekly downward spikes, lows within ~$0.25, large overlap, spikes far longer than the year's typical spikes, week before/after back near the pipe top, best at the end of downtrends; 442 found in 200 stocks): failure 12%, avg rise 47% (from the average of the high and low of the week AFTER the pipe to the ultimate high; 60% from the right pipe low), likely 20% (10-40% flat), 39% >50%, ~9.5 months to the high. Daily-chart pipes were worse (failure 18%, avg 33%, likely 10%). Author notes he picked parameters afterwards but 'verified in out-of-sample tests'. Stop ~half a point below the pipe low (retests are common). [Note: 'ultimate high' measure + hindsight identification.]
## Ch31 Pipe top (443 in 150 stocks): failure 18%; avg decline 21% (likely 10-20%); ~4 months; mostly a consolidation (54%); best in downtrends (3-month downtrend before: decline 24%, failure 11%); in uptrends it often marks 1-2 months of weakness.
## Ch32 Rectangle bottom (95; price trend into the box is DOWN, horizontal top/bottom lines with >=2 touches each): up-break 41 (reversal) failure low, avg +46%, likely 20%, target (box height added) hit 93%; down-break 54 (consolidation) failure 4%, avg -19% (likely 10-15%), target hit 65%; ~3 months; throwback 61% / pullback 70%; premature breakouts (close outside, then back in) occur 12-17% and the real breakout is on the OPPOSITE side 75% of the time; volume trend only slightly informative. Tactics: wait for the close outside the lines; trade the range if tall; use pullbacks/throwbacks.
## Ch33 Rectangle top (~200; uptrend into a box): mostly consolidations (breaks UP): up-break failure 2%, avg +52% (likely 20-30%), measure rule hit 91%; down-break failure 0% (none found; sample caveat), avg -20% (likely 20%), hit 65%; ~3 months; premature breakouts (close outside then back) are on the opposite side of the real break 65% (up-premature -> real down 57%; down-premature -> real up 71%); receding volume slightly better for up-breaks (55% vs 48%); ~8 months to the high for up-breaks. Tactics: wait for a close outside the lines, trade in the trend direction, scalp the range if tall.
## Ch34 Rounding bottom (saucer; weekly; 243): bowl shape over months, often a handle; failure 38% by his classification (reversals count as failures) but 5% if you wait for the close above the left lip/right lip; avg rise 54% (likely 20%; 25% >90%); measure rule only 36%; ~8.5 months long; volume often bowl-shaped. Buy only after the close above the right saucer lip/handle high.
## Ch35 Rounding top (dome; 165): not bearish - 61% break UP above the dome high; failure 19% (6% if you wait for the up-break); avg +41% (likely 20-40%) to the high ~1 year later; measure rule (dome height added) 69%; ~6 months long; 95% of the breakouts near the yearly high. Buy the close above the dome high.
## Ch36 Scallops (J-shape, ascending / reversed-J descending; ~613 / ~414): ascending: failure 25%, avg +33% (likely 20-30%), target hit 71%, ~57 days, consolidation mostly; descending: failure 3%, avg -24% (likely 20%), target hit 52%; consecutive scallops get smaller (2nd 67% of the time, 8th 1%).
## Ch36 tail (scallops): measure rule uses the RIGHT-edge high (so harder for ascending scallops); descending scallops form late in downtrends, careful shorting them far into a decline. Stops: ascending - below the bowl low; descending - above the right lip high.
## Ch37 SHARK-32 (3-day symmetric triangle: consecutive lower highs and higher lows; ~300 found in 100 stocks): breakout when price closes beyond the FIRST (widest) day's high/low, ~8 days later. Failure ~41% up / 44% down; avg +32% (likely 10-15%) / -21% (likely <10%); throwback/pullback 64%/58%; better when the three days are symmetric about the horizontal axis; acts as a midpoint ('half-mast') formation. Author: do not trade.
## Ch38 ASCENDING TRIANGLE (725; flat top with >=2 minor highs, rising lower line with >=2 minor lows, price should cross the pattern several times; volume heavy early, light before the breakout): 73% consolidations; breaks up 62%, down 33%, horizontal ~5%. Failure 32% overall (2% if you wait for the up-close; 5% failures); up-break avg +44% (likely 20%, 49% <30%); failed ones fall avg 21%; measure rule (height at the start added to the top line) hit 89% but average height is only ~10% of price so the target is easy; breakout on average 63% of the way to the apex (strongest breakouts ~2/3 of the way); premature breakouts 25% (22% up, 12% down; volume as heavy as genuine ones; they do NOT predict the final direction); throwback 58%, pullback 49%; ~64 days to the breakout; ~6 months to the high. Tactics: buy the day after a close above the top line (or the throwback); sell near the target; stop under the formation low; raise to break-even after +10%; if short, cover on an up-break.
## Ch39 DESCENDING TRIANGLE (689): flat bottom, falling top line. Only 55% break down (45% failure; 4% if you wait for the close below the base); down-break avg -19% (likely 10-20%), target hit 67%; up-breaks avg +42%! (trade the breakout direction); premature breakouts rare (22%); pullback 64%; ~61 days to the break, 87 to the apex. Tactics: never short before the break; trade with the trend.
## Ch40-41 SYMMETRICAL TRIANGLE (coil): two converging sloping lines (neither horizontal), >=2 minor highs and >=2 minor lows touching them, price should cross the pattern repeatedly (no big empty centre), volume recedes, >3 weeks long (<=3 weeks = pennant); a pattern that sits inside a head-and-shoulders, diamond or double/triple top should be read as that bigger pattern. Classified 'bottom' (prices fell into it) vs 'top' (prices rose into it) - the split did NOT predict breakout direction. Breakout is LATE: ~75-85% of the way to the apex (not 2/3). Premature breakouts 16-19%, indistinguishable by volume, slightly earlier (71-76% to apex).
 - Bottoms (146): up-break 63 reversals - failure 3%, avg +41% (likely 20%, 3/4 of cases >20%), target hit 79%; down-break 83 consolidations - failure 2%, avg -19% (likely 10%), target 57%; ~2 months; pullback 57%, throwback 43%; 5.5 months to the high.
 - Tops (255): up-break 162 consolidations - failure 5%, avg +37% (likely 20%), target 81%; down-break 93 reversals - failure 6%, avg -20% (likely 15%), target 62%; throwback/pullback 58-59% (~12 days); 6.5 months to the high; high-volume up-breaks +38% vs +35% low volume (n.s.); high-volume down-breaks slightly bigger.
 - Measure rule: formation height at its widest added to the high/subtracted from the low (or a parallel-line projection). Trading inside the triangle is possible because the break comes so late.
## Ch42 TRIPLE BOTTOM (122; rare): three well separated lows at about the same price (centre not much lower - else H&S), rallies between them; CONFIRMATION POINT = highest high in the formation; not a triple bottom until price closes above it (avg 42 days after the third low; up to 134 days, 13 within 10 days). Failure 4%; avg rise 38% (likely 20%, 10-50% flat); measure rule hit 73%; ~4 months long (61 and 60 days between lows); throwback 70% (9 days); ~7.5 months to the high. Volume: only 28% show the textbook falling volume on each low and those did WORSE (28% vs 38%); a lower-volume centre bottom vs the last bottom did better (40% vs 35%). Buying before confirmation usually fails (price resumes the old trend/ flat). Stop below the lowest low.
## Ch43 TRIPLE TOP (122): three peaks at similar price (centre not much higher), confirmation = the lowest low in the formation; failure 15%; avg decline 21% (likely 10%); measure rule only 47%; ~4 months, ~40 days from the last top to the confirmation close; pullback 84%(!) in ~8 days; third top HIGHER than the second -> bigger loss (22% vs 17%, significant); 31% have a lower centre peak; ~3.5 months to the low. Without the confirming close, in bull markets, price soars away. Wait for confirmation and the pullback; stop above the highest high.
## Ch44 FALLING WEDGE (125; rare): both lines slope down, upper steeper, converge; >=5 touches (3+2), >=3 weeks (usually 1.5 months avg 44 days), volume falling (70%). Failure 10% (2% if you wait for the up-close); 92% break up; avg rise 43% (likely 20-30%, many >90%); target = just above the top of the wedge (88% - an easy bar); premature breakouts 27% (look like genuine ones); throwback 47%; strongest breakouts at 50-80% of the way to the apex; ~7 months to the high. 62% are consolidations in uptrends; ~10 of 12 failures are breaks down inside a large head-and-shoulders.

## Ch46 Weekly reversal DOWN (higher high, lower low, close below the prior week's LOW, in an uptrend near its peak; 412 in 200 stocks): failure 37%; avg decline 18% (likely 10%; 51% <15%); measure rule (that week's range subtracted from the close) 73%; ~80 days; failures more likely the further the signal is from the peak (>3-5 weeks); wide weekly range (>10-15% of price) improves results (6% -> 24% avg loss across range buckets); volume effect unclear. ## Ch47 Weekly reversal UP (higher high, lower low, close above the prior week's HIGH, in a downtrend; 484): failure 23%; avg +44% (likely 20%; 17% >90%; 37% <20%); measure rule 84%; ~7 months to the high; wide weekly range better (15% -> 57% avg gain across buckets); volume 1.5x average best (44% vs 38% for low volume) but higher volume deteriorates (2x: 44, 3x: 38, 4x: 29).
## Statistics-summary caveat (author): measured-move up/down averages (68% / -36%) are unrealistic because they start at the pattern's start; realistic ~37% and ~-20%.
## Cross-pattern take-aways the author himself states
 - Almost every pattern needs a CLOSE beyond the boundary; failure rates fall sharply (often to 2-10%) if you wait for that confirmation - because the confirmation conditions on the outcome (a double top that never closes below its valley is simply not counted as a double top).
 - Throwbacks/pullbacks occur after roughly half to 70-80% of confirmed breakouts within ~10-15 days.
 - Average gains are inflated by the 'ultimate high' measure and by outliers; the 'most likely' (modal) move is typically 10-20%.
 - Volume rules (high on left, low on right, breakout surge) are weak or unhelpful in his own tests; in several tests the opposite held.
 - Trend/context matters: most patterns act as consolidations of the prior trend (bull market 1991-96 bias).
 - Measure-rule hit rates are usually 40-70%; he reaches '80%+' only by shrinking targets (half height) after the fact.
